How this is measured

  • When: each notice's first publication time on Upbit, not its last edit. Amended notices are re-stamped hours later; timing from the edit would start after the market had already moved.
  • Price: Binance spot, one-second bars, in USDT. A price at time t is the close of the last bar that had finished by t, so nothing reads the future.
  • Move: the coin's return minus BTC's over the same window, so a market-wide move isn't credited to the notice. The chart and the trade use the coin's own price.
  • Trade: long a listing or a lifted caution; short a delisting, caution or warning. Filled a given number of seconds after the notice, closed after the hold, taker fee on each side. Worst fill is the bar's extreme against you in the second your order arrives (its high for a buy); fair is the last trade price, which flatters a fast market. A 0 s fair fill is the price before the notice: the size of the whole move, which no one gets.
  • Read the median. A few listings that doubled carry the mean; the typical event is the median.
  • What's missing: coins not trading on Binance at the time (a large share of Upbit listings), order-book depth, and whether a short was even possible. Small groups prove little: every chart shows how many events it rests on.
